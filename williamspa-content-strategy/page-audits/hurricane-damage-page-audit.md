**H2: Why Hurricane Claims Are Denied in Florida**

**Wind vs. water misclassification**
The most common hurricane claim dispute in Florida. Standard homeowners policies cover wind damage but not flood damage. Insurers frequently attribute damage to storm surge or rising water — even when wind was the clear cause — to avoid paying. We retain forensic meteorologists and engineers to establish causation.

**"Pre-existing damage" allegations**
Adjusters inspect after storms and frequently label damage as pre-existing. We document storm timing, weather data, and inspection records to challenge this.

**Underpayment via lowball estimates**
Even approved claims are often paid far below actual repair costs. Insurers use in-house estimators who undervalue scope and materials. We obtain independent contractor estimates and fight for full replacement cost.

**Hurricane deductible disputes**
Florida policies carry a separate hurricane deductible — typically 2%–10% of your dwelling coverage — that only triggers when the Governor declares a hurricane emergency. Insurers sometimes apply it when they shouldn't, or dispute whether the trigger was met.

**Proof of loss disputes**
Insurers may reject your proof of loss as incomplete or untimely to toll their own deadlines. We manage the proof of loss process to ensure full compliance.

---

**H2: Florida Law and Your Rights**

**Filing deadlines — do not miss these:**
- **1 year** from the date of loss to file a new claim or reopen a prior claim
- **3 years** from the date of loss to file a lawsuit (Florida Statute §627.70132)

**Insurer obligations:**
- Must acknowledge your claim within 14 days (§627.70131)
- Must pay or deny within 90 days of receiving proof of loss (§627.70131)

**Bad faith:**
If your insurer unreasonably denies or delays your claim, you may be entitled to damages beyond the policy limit under Florida's bad faith statute (§624.155). We file Civil Remedy Notices and pursue bad faith claims where warranted.

**H2: What to Do After Hurricane Damage in Florida**

1. **Document immediately** — Photograph and video all damage before any cleanup or repairs. Capture rooflines, interiors, and the source of every water pathway.
2. **Mitigate** — Your policy requires you to prevent additional damage. Tarp open roofs, extract standing water, move valuables. Keep receipts for all mitigation costs.
3. **Report your claim promptly** — File within your policy's reporting window and track every insurer communication in writing.
4. **Don't give a recorded statement without legal advice** — [Read why here.](/blog/should-i-give-a-recorded-statement-to-an-insurance-company/)
5. **Don't sign a release or accept a settlement without review** — Once signed, your claim is closed. Call us first.
6. **Contact Williams Law** — Free evaluation, contingency fee, no upfront costs.

**H2: Frequently Asked Questions**

**What is a hurricane deductible and when does it apply?**
A hurricane deductible is a separate, higher deductible in your Florida homeowners policy — typically 2%–10% of your dwelling coverage — that applies only when the Governor officially declares a hurricane emergency and your damage is caused by that named storm. On a $400,000 home with a 2% deductible, you pay the first $8,000. [Full explanation →](/faq/what-is-a-hurricane-deductible-and-when-does-it-apply/)

**What is the deadline to file a hurricane insurance claim in Florida?**
You have **one year** from the date of loss to file a new claim or reopen a prior claim. You have **three years** from the date of loss to file a lawsuit under Florida Statute §627.70132. Missing either deadline forfeits your rights.

**My insurer says my damage is from flooding, not wind. What can I do?**
Wind vs. water causation disputes are the #1 reason hurricane claims are denied in Florida. This is a disputed factual and legal question — not a final answer. We work with meteorologists and structural engineers to establish the true cause of your damage and challenge the insurer's classification.

**What common mistakes should I avoid after a hurricane?**
The most costly mistakes are: waiting too long to file, failing to document before cleanup, accepting a lowball first payment, signing a release without legal review, and giving a recorded statement to the insurer's adjuster. [Full guide →](/faq/what-are-common-mistakes-to-avoid-when-filing-a-hurricane-insurance-claim/)

**Do you handle commercial hurricane claims?**
Yes. We represent businesses, landlords, HOAs, and condominium associations for hurricane damage claims, including business interruption losses and large commercial property disputes.

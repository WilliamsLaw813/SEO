### H2: Why Florida Insurers Deny Water Damage Claims

Insurance companies use several standard tactics to deny or minimise water damage claims. Knowing them helps you respond — and helps us fight back.

**"Gradual deterioration" or "long-term seepage"**
Florida homeowners policies cover *sudden and accidental* water damage, but exclude damage insurers classify as gradual. Adjusters often exploit this by claiming a slow leak was long-standing — even when there's no evidence. We challenge these determinations with independent experts.

**"Lack of maintenance"**
Insurers frequently deny claims by arguing the homeowner failed to maintain their plumbing, roof, or appliances. This is one of the most abused exclusions in Florida property law.

**"Pre-existing damage"**
An adjuster may claim the damage existed before your policy period. We subpoena inspection records, prior claims history, and contractor reports to refute this.

**Lowball estimates**
Even when a claim is approved, the payout is often far below the true cost to repair. Insurance companies use in-house estimators incentivised to keep valuations low.

**Flood vs. water damage misclassification**
Standard homeowners policies do not cover flooding (rising water from outside the home). Insurers sometimes misclassify storm-driven water intrusion as flooding to avoid paying. If your claim was denied on this basis, call us — the distinction often comes down to the direction the water travelled, and it's frequently wrong.

### H2: Florida Law and Your Rights

**Sudden and accidental coverage**
Most Florida homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental. Florida courts have consistently held that insurers bear the burden of proving an exclusion applies — not the other way around.

**Florida Statute §627.70131 — the 90-day rule**
Your insurer must pay or deny your claim within 90 days of receiving proof of loss. Failure to do so can support a bad faith claim.

**Florida Statute §624.155 — bad faith**
If your insurer unreasonably delays or denies your claim, you may be entitled to damages beyond the policy limit under Florida's bad faith insurance statute. We file Civil Remedy Notices (CRNs) and pursue bad faith claims where warranted.

**One-year deadline**
Under Florida's 2023 insurance reforms, you have **one year** from the date of loss to file a new claim and **18 months** to reopen a prior claim. Do not wait.

### H2: What to Do After Water Damage in Florida

Acting quickly protects both your property and your claim.

1. **Stop the source** — Turn off the water supply if possible. Document where the leak originated before repairs.
2. **Document everything** — Photograph and video all damage before any cleanup or mitigation. Capture the source, affected materials, and water pathways.
3. **Mitigate further damage** — You have a duty under your policy to prevent additional damage. Extract standing water, place tarps over roof openings, move valuables.
4. **Report to your insurer** — File your claim promptly. Keep a written record of every communication.
5. **Do not give a recorded statement without legal advice** — Insurers use recorded statements to build denial arguments. [Read why here.](/blog/should-i-give-a-recorded-statement-to-an-insurance-company/)
6. **Do not accept a lowball settlement** — Once you sign a release, your claim is closed. Call us first.
7. **Contact Williams Law** — We offer a free case evaluation and handle all water damage claims on contingency.

### H2: Frequently Asked Questions

**Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Florida?**
Most standard Florida homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — such as a burst pipe or roof leak from a storm. Gradual leaks, flooding from outside the home, and maintenance neglect are typically excluded. The line between covered and excluded damage is often disputed by insurers. If you've been denied, contact us for a free review.

**My insurance company said my water damage is from "gradual deterioration." What can I do?**
This is one of the most common denial tactics in Florida. "Gradual deterioration" is often applied broadly and incorrectly. We work with independent forensic experts to establish when damage actually began and challenge the insurer's characterisation. Many successfully denied claims are overturned on this basis.

**What is the difference between water damage and flood damage?**
Water damage typically refers to water that originates inside the structure (pipe burst, appliance leak, roof leak). Flood damage refers to water rising from outside — a storm surge, overflowing river, or heavy rain runoff. Standard homeowners policies cover water damage but not flooding; flood coverage requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy. Insurers sometimes misclassify storm water intrusion as flooding to avoid paying — this is a common dispute we handle.

**How long do I have to file a water damage claim in Florida?**
Under Florida's current insurance laws, you have **one year** from the date of loss to file a new claim and **18 months** to reopen or supplement a prior claim. These deadlines are strict. If you're approaching a deadline, call us immediately.

**What if my water damage claim was already denied?**
A denial is not final. We review denied claims at no cost and frequently identify grounds to dispute the denial — including improper exclusion application, inadequate investigation, or bad faith conduct. Contact us before accepting the denial.

**Do I need a lawyer or a public adjuster for my water damage claim?**
A public adjuster negotiates the value of your claim but cannot sue your insurer if they refuse to pay fairly. An attorney can do both — and can pursue bad faith damages if the insurer has acted wrongfully. For disputed or denied claims, an attorney typically delivers better results.
